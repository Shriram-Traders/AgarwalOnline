# Implementation status

This ledger describes the launch-first implementation. The complete website flow is present; provider-backed production acceptance still depends on credentials and operating decisions.

## Governance and access

- Four fixed roles are enforced on the server: customer, delivery, admin and super-admin.
- Better Auth provides MongoDB-backed customer phone OTP, staff email/password authentication, signed cookies, session lifecycle and endpoint rate limiting. Existing credential hashes migrate into Better Auth accounts on first staff sign-in.
- Super Admin can create, edit, deactivate and reassign staff, revoke all staff sessions and inspect the visible permission matrix.
- Product, price and large stock changes use independent approval, rejection reasons, stale-value protection and scheduled publication.
- Searchable audit history records staff, access, configuration, inventory, delivery, evidence and refund actions. Sensitive fields are recursively redacted in logs and the UI.
- Active Better Auth sessions, ownership and permissions are checked for every protected page, action, route and chat poll. The legacy session fallback was removed on 2026-09-23; Better Auth is the only session source.

## Customer website

- Responsive home, catalog, product, basket, checkout, payment, order tracking, profile, address, wishlist and notification journeys.
- Catalog and navigation support English and Marathi. Homepage collections use catalog, inventory and customer purchase data.
- Saved addresses can be created, edited and deleted. Reorder revalidates live variants, availability and price before adding items.
- Authenticated invoice downloads and customer-owned complaint, evidence, support and return/refund flows are implemented.
- COD and Razorpay share transaction-safe inventory reservation, slot capacity, idempotency and immutable order snapshots.

## Store operations

- Category, product, variant, image and inventory management with approval boundaries and inventory history.
- Authenticated JPG/PNG/WebP upload path with signed Cloudinary storage and an ignored local mock adapter. Photos are shrunk in the browser (1600px JPEG) before upload; the server takes up to 3.5 MB (4 MB server-action body limit). Product photos never expire; other evidence follows `EVIDENCE_RETENTION_DAYS`.
- Packing quantities, substitutions and photographs; scoped delivery assignment, attempt reasons, delivery evidence and OTP completion.
- The packing checklist shows the customer's "If an item is unavailable" choice and opens as it was last saved. A line packed short needs a word about the rest: not available, the substitute packed at the same price (a product's name; notes like "none" or "1 short" are refused), or both with a count. Once every line has one, the order follows what was packed: each line keeps what was ordered and reads "Not available – not charged" or "Substituted with …" on the customer's, staff and rider's order pages and the invoice; stock set aside for units that didn't go in goes back on sale, and staff are asked to fix the stock count for them (and for substitutes); a cash-on-delivery bill is worked out again (offer recalculated on the terms saved at checkout, delivery fee kept), so the rider collects the new total; and the customer is told. An order paid online keeps its bill and payment as they are: the shortfall is recorded on the order, the owners are told what is owed, it sits on their work queue ("Refunds owed for items not packed") until a refund is made from the Refunds page, and the order pages and invoice say once it has been. A checklist finished under the older rules needs one more save before "Mark packed". Only confirming and completing an order message the customer; picking, packed and ready are the shop's own steps.
- Staff can cancel an order until it leaves the shop (reason required; its stock, slot and offer are freed once), send a delivery that didn't go through out again or close it as returned to shop, and change or remove the rider before the delivery starts or after a missed attempt. A rider whose order is taken off them gets a message to bring the parcel back, and deliveries that didn't go through have their own filter and work-queue row on the dashboard. Orders still holding an online payment are never closed from the order page: the refund is handled separately on the Refunds page first, and a cancelled order whose online payment came in late says so, with who refunds it.
- COD collection, handover, discrepancy tracking and resolution stay separate from delivery status.
- Return progress and partial/full refunds are recorded through provider-aware state, audit and customer notifications. Webhooks reconcile Razorpay payment and refund events.
- Persistent support chat polls an authenticated route, with sequence-based replay, retry deduplication, receipts, typing, assignment and internal notes.

## Reporting and operations

- Date-filtered sales, gross/net revenue, refunds, order/payment status, top product, service area, low-stock and delivery performance views.
- Admin customer views mask phone numbers and expose only operational purchase context.
- Seeded analytics are labeled fictional in the UI.
- Structured JSON logging redacts sensitive keys. Environment startup validates grouped provider variables and retention settings.
- Reservation expiry, scheduled approvals and evidence/notification/audit retention have runnable job scripts.

## Provider boundary

- Razorpay, Cloudinary, SMS and Atlas Search have production-shaped configuration points.
- Without credentials, payments do not claim live processing, uploads use local mock storage, OTP uses the development mock only when explicitly enabled, and search uses a bounded MongoDB fallback.
- Live merchant, SMS and Cloudinary acceptance must be completed with the operator's own accounts before launch.

Pale amber `#fef3c7` carries the second offer card, cool grey `#f5f6f8` the wells, search field and chips.
- Signature details: a dark ink announcement strip carrying the amber coupon chip; a split hero where the photo holds the right edge under a fade mask with a faint dot grid behind the copy; the delivery and payment facts rendered as hairline-divided cells; product cards as hairline boxes with a navy ticket-shaped discount tag (notched left edge), an outlined navy ADD that becomes a solid navy stepper, and a price row ruled off with a hairline.
- A six-agent audit of the re-theme (landing, catalogue and product, basket and forms, contrast, responsive, Marathi) raised 48 findings; the confirmed ones are fixed. The substantive ones were a leftover espresso `--warning` brown still tinting notices and rating stars, a navy focus ring that was invisible on the dark announcement strip at 1.72:1, placeholder text at 3.04:1, control borders at 1.74:1 against the 3:1 the guidelines ask for, touch targets of 32 to 36px on phones, the sticky basket bar sitting on the footer's last row, the promo form's button dropping below the field once a code was rejected, and the savings line inheriting link navy instead of success green.
- It replaced "Paper & Ink, warmed" (Instrument Serif on oat with espresso and marigold), which had been the approved look since 19 September. The swap was almost entirely a `:root` token diff plus the font change, which is what the stylesheet was built for.
- One token-based stylesheet, `src/app/globals.css`. Base colours are a handful of tokens; tints, shades, neutrals, the staff shell and the focus ring derive from them with relative colour syntax and `color-mix()`. Every page styles itself through the same semantic classes, so account, checkout, staff and admin screens follow the storefront automatically.
- Header: wordmark, compact search, delivery area, sign-in and basket on the first row; a second row lists a fixed set of aisle groups whose labels come from the catalogue (scrolls horizontally on tablets, hidden on phones where the bottom tab bar has Aisles); the offer strip shows the free-delivery threshold and the live welcome code from the promotions collection. Footer links are generated from the catalogue.
- Fonts are self-hosted through `next/font/local`: Plus Jakarta Sans for headings (700-800, tracking -0.025em), Manrope for body, and **Mukta for Devanagari**. Latin never reaches Mukta because it sits behind Manrope in the stack and the Latin faces carry no Devanagari glyphs, so Marathi finally renders in a real face instead of a system fallback.
- Guests can add to basket from product pages; basket lines use a quantity stepper with remove; checkout is a two-column form with a sticky order summary. Home and catalog copy is bilingual and follows the saved locale cookie.

- Quick-commerce patterns (borrowed from Zepto, Blinkit and Instamart, keeping our palette): delivery promise over the area in the phone header; search box cycles example queries; offer and delivery banners sit directly under the header (a swipe rail on phones); phone aisles are a four-column tile grid and phone product sections are swipe rails; the editorial hero is desktop-only; product cards use an outlined ADD that becomes an in-place ink stepper (`QuickAdd` calls the quick-add and set-quantity actions directly); a sticky "N items · total · View basket" bar (`CartBar`) appears on phones whenever the basket has items, except on basket, checkout and staff routes. Discount tags stay marigold, the equivalent of Blinkit's yellow tags.
- Customer sign-up now asks for the password twice and can reveal it. The confirm field blocks submission natively through `setCustomValidity`, and `verifyOTPAction` re-checks the match on the server because the client is not the authority. Every password box in the auth form carries a reveal toggle with an `aria-pressed` label. The customer minimum moved from 12 characters to 8, which matches the NIST SP 800-63B floor for user-chosen passwords. Staff passwords were kept at 12 at first, then lowered to 8 on 2026-09-23 at the owner's request, so every account now uses the same 8-character minimum.
- Sign-in accepted only one spelling of the local origin. `checkOrigin` compared the request's `Origin` header to `APP_ORIGIN` as an exact string, so with `APP_ORIGIN=http://127.0.0.1:3000` every auth action failed from `http://localhost:3000` — the URL Next prints on startup — and the masked message read "Unable to sign in. Please try again later." `src/lib/auth/origin.ts` now treats `localhost` and `127.0.0.1` as the same host **only when the configured origin is itself loopback**, and Better Auth's `trustedOrigins` gets both spellings. `env.ts` requires a production origin to be https, so a deployment can never reach that branch; `tests/origin.test.ts` covers the cross-site cases including a host that merely contains the configured one.
- End-to-end coverage: `tests/e2e/journey.spec.ts` drives the whole flow in one pass — sign up with OTP, search, add to basket, raise the quantity with the stepper, apply a promotion code, save an address, check out on cash, then confirm, pick, pack, assign, deliver with the customer's code and reconcile the cash. It also watches the browser and fails on any console error, page error, failed request or 5xx response, and writes full-page screenshots of every populated screen when `SHOT_DIR` is set. The per-surface specs start from empty fixtures, so this is the only test that exercises populated lists, tables and status strips.

Each maps onto CSS that already existed rather than introducing new class names.

The admin order detail, which had no heading block at all, gained one.

- Landing page (September 2026 rebuild, after studying BigBasket, Swiggy Instamart and Tesco at 1440px): none of them uses a full-screen editorial photo hero, because it pushes every shoppable element below the fold. Ours was doing exactly that, so the hero became a compact **welcome band** carrying the things a first-time visitor actually needs: what the shop is, a live countdown to the same-day cutoff, and an inline PIN serviceability check that works without JavaScript. A four-photo mosaic keeps the editorial tone without eating the screen. The store has no published reviews and few orders, so there are deliberately no ratings, review counts or "customers served" claims.

## Schools & quotations (October 2026)

Schools buy in bulk by quotation, not through checkout. Nothing here takes payment or books delivery; an accepted quotation is followed up by the store outside the site.

- **Who sees a product.** Each product has two ticks, Customers (`showToCustomers`, default on) and Schools (`showToSchools`, default off), set on its staff page. Every shopper read goes through `shopperVisible`/`isShopperVisible` in `src/lib/catalog/visibility.ts`, so a schools-only item never reaches the catalogue, product page, basket, checkout, lists or wishlist. The school catalogue is `catalogForSchools`, which takes only a search and an aisle: the audience is never read from the address.
- **School price and GST.** A pack can carry an optional school price (`schoolPricePaise`, before GST, owner-only). Representatives see it as "Expected ₹X + GST (r%)", or "Price on quotation". Products carry a GST rate (`GST_RATES` in `src/lib/tax/gst.ts`: 0, 5, 18, 40, to be confirmed with the CA) and an optional HSN code. The shop's legal name, GSTIN, address, state and quotation terms are in Store settings ("Business and tax details").
- **Schools and representatives** (`src/lib/schools/`). The owner adds a school at `/super-admin/schools`, then adds people who already have an account, or shares the school's join link (`/school/join/<token>`). People who open the link sign in and ask for access, and the owner approves or declines. A representative is an ordinary customer account plus a `SchoolMember` row; sign-in is unchanged. Several representatives per school, and a person may represent several schools. Pausing a school shuts its area; a new link stops the old one.
- **Privacy between schools.** Every representative read and write checks membership (`assertMember`, `schoolContext`). Another school's id behaves as if it didn't exist (not found), so no school sees another's basket, requests, quotations or prices.
- **Shared basket and request.** One `QuoteBasket` per school, shared by its representatives (each line shows who added it). Its `rev` counter refuses a send from a basket someone else just changed. "Ask for a quotation" freezes the items into a `QuoteRequest` numbered `AGSQ-YYYYMMDD-00001` (its own `Sequence` key, apart from order numbers), empties the basket and notifies the owners.
- **Pricing desk** (`/super-admin/quotations/[id]`, `settings:write`). One row per item asked for, starting at the school price or the shop price with GST taken out (`startingUnitPrice`). The owner edits quantity (0 leaves it off), rate before GST, GST rate and HSN, plus a discount (% or ₹, spread over lines by largest remainder), a valid-until date (default 15 days) and a note. Totals update live with the same `quoteTotals` the server stores: tax per rate group, half-up to the paisa, CGST + SGST within Maharashtra and IGST for another state. Sending is held while the sheet has unsaved changes and refused if the draft changed since the page opened.
- **Sending and answers.** Each send freezes a new version with seller and buyer snapshots, so later price or detail changes never alter what a school saw. Representatives get a notification and, if they have a real email address, an email; the owner's message counts emailed, no address and failed. The school opens `/school/quotations/[id]`, prints or saves it as PDF, and either accepts the current, unexpired version or asks for changes with a note; the owner revises and sends the next version. Accepted and closed requests are final.
- **View-only link.** Every quotation request has its own `shareToken`: 24 random characters (`randomBytes(18)`, base64url), unrelated to its `AGSQ` number, date or database id, so one link can't be worked out from another. `/q/<code>` shows the latest sent version to anyone with the link, signed in or not, with print or save as PDF; there are no answer buttons, and the school's notes and the owner's draft never show. Accepting or asking for changes still needs a representative signed in at `/school/quotations/[id]`. The quotation email links to `/q/<code>`, so it opens for whoever it is forwarded to; in-app notifications keep the signed-in page. The share box is on the school's quotation page (once a version is sent) and on the owner's desk, where **Make a new link** (`resetQuoteLink`, audit `quote.link.reset`, the code itself kept out of the history) stops the old link at once. Requests from before links get their code when first shown or sent (`quoteShareToken`). The page is `noindex` and `/q/` is disallowed in `robots.ts`.
- **Checks.** `tests/quote-math.test.ts`, `gst.test.ts`, `quote-form.test.ts`, `visibility`, `tax-profile`, `schools` and `quotes` integration tests, and `tests/e2e/school.spec.ts` (the whole round trip at desktop and phone width, including another school's representative getting "not found").

## School marketplace (October 2026)

The school area became its own marketplace that shops like the store: same navy look, its own header, and one difference at the end. Checkout says **Create quotation** instead of taking payment. Everything about quotations above still holds; only the pages around them changed.

- **Chrome.** `AdaptiveShell` leaves `/school/**` (`isSchoolPath`, `src/lib/schools/paths.ts`) to `src/app/school/layout.tsx`. That layout renders `SchoolHeader`, built on the shop's `.header` grid: logo "for schools", the school being bought for (a switcher for people in several), a search of the school catalogue only (`SmartSearch` pointed at `/school/catalog` and the members-only `/api/school/suggestions`), the account menu with the language switch, and a basket icon counting the school's shared basket. The aisles row is built from `schoolAisles()`, with "Shop for home". Phones get `SchoolTabs`: Home, Aisles, Basket, Quotations, You.
- **Which school.** The page address no longer carries `?s=`. The chosen school is a preference cookie (`ags_school`), set only by `GET /school/use?s=&then=`, which checks membership and only goes on to `/school` pages. `schoolContext(user, wanted, preferred)` uses `?s=` first, then the cookie, then the only school. A stale choice is ignored, while a foreign `?s=` is still "not found". Old links that carry `?s=` (notifications and emails) pass through `/school/use`, so the header and the page agree.
- **Pages.**
  - `/school` is a home like the shop's: welcome band, the basket or a ready quotation beside it, latest quotations, aisle tiles, "Popular with schools" and how it works.
  - `/school/catalog` shows photo cards or a bulk list with quantity boxes. The Cards/List links go through `/school/view`, which remembers the choice in an `ags_school_view` cookie.
  - `/school/products/[slug]` is the product page. It uses `schoolProductBySlug`: only items ticked for schools, and only for representatives. Its title is fixed, so a school-only name can't leak.
  - `/school/basket` mirrors `/cart`: expected subtotal, GST estimated with `estimateQuote` (`src/lib/schools/estimate.ts`, the same rounding as the quotation), and items still to be priced.
  - `/school/checkout` shows who the quotation is for (the School record), an optional needed-by date, a note, and the **Create quotation** button, which is the existing `submitQuoteRequest`.

Customers rate an order after delivery; only the shop reads it. Product reviews stay the public ones.

- **One row per order.** `OrderFeedback` (`src/lib/feedback/models.ts`) is keyed by `orderId` and has no customer id: who may write or read it is decided through the order. - **The popup.** The header asks `pendingFeedbackOrder` for the shopper's newest delivered order of the last 14 days with no row; staff accounts are skipped. `FeedbackPrompt` opens the sheet about a second after a page settles, only on pages for browsing (`feedbackPromptAllowed` in `src/lib/feedback/rules.ts`), never over another popup. - **Zepto-style sheet** (`src/components/feedback-sheet.tsx`). One tap on a star is enough. ## Install as an app (October 2026)

Chrome offers **Install** for the shop: the install icon in the address bar on a computer, or **Install app** / **Add to Home screen** in the menu on Android. The app opens in its own window, starting at the home page, with the shop's icon.

- **Manifest** (`src/app/manifest.ts`, served as `/manifest.webmanifest`): name "Agarwal General Stores", short name "Agarwal", standalone window, the browser-bar colour, and shortcuts to Shop all, Basket and My orders on a long press of the icon.
- **Icons.** A navy square with a white "A" and the amber bar. `src/app/icon.svg` is the browser-tab icon and the master drawing; `public/icons/icon-192.png` and `icon-512.png` are the rounded app icons, `maskable-192.png` and `maskable-512.png` are full-bleed copies with the letter inside the safe zone for phones that cut their own shape, and `src/app/apple-icon.png` (180×180) is for an iPhone home screen (`appleWebApp` in the root layout).
- **Service worker** (`public/sw.js`, registered by `src/components/service-worker.tsx`). It only steps in when a page is opened (GET navigations): the page still comes from the network, and only with no connection at all does `public/offline.html` show instead of the browser's error. Nothing the shop shows is cached, so prices, stock, the basket and sign-in are always live. Sign-in pages and callbacks (`/login`, `/signup`, `/staff/login`, `/auth/…`, `/api/…`) never pass through it, and there is no navigation preload, which would request a one-time sign-in callback twice. To change what it does, edit `sw.js` and bump `CACHE`; browsers pick up the new worker on their next visit.
- **Where it works.** Install needs https, or `localhost` on the PC itself. `npm run phone` uses a self-signed certificate, so phones on the Wi-Fi may not offer install there; the live https site will.
- **Checks.** `tests/manifest.test.ts`: the manifest has what Chrome needs, every icon exists at its stated size, and the worker leaves sign-in alone and stores only the offline notice. Chrome's own check (`Page.getInstallabilityErrors` over the DevTools protocol) reported no problems against the dev server.

## Push notifications (October 2026)

Everything that lands in someone's bell (`notify()` in `src/lib/engagement/service.ts`) is also pushed to the phones and PCs where they turned notifications on, so it arrives even when the shop isn't open. Staff who manage orders (`order:manage`) also get **New order AGS-… · ₹1,240 · 3 items · Kalpana S. · Nagothane · cash on delivery** in their bell and as a push the moment an order is placed (`notifyNewOrder`, called from `createOrder`); tapping it opens the order in the workspace. This adds to the in-page new-order chime, which only works while a staff tab is open.

- **Turning it on.** A "Notifications on this device" card (`src/components/push-settings.tsx`) on `/account` and `/account/notifications#push` (also in the workspace search). Chrome's permission prompt only appears after **Turn on notifications** is tapped. Once on, **Send a test** and **Turn off**. A blocked site gets the way to unblock it; an iPhone is told to add the shop to its Home Screen first (Apple only allows push there).
- **Keys.** Push is off until `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY` and `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` are set (`npm run push:keys` makes a pair; `.env.example` explains). Make the pair once and use the same one locally and on the live site: a new pair means every device turns notifications on again. `VAPID_SUBJECT` is optional; the live https address is used when it's blank.
- **Sending.** `src/lib/push/web-push.ts` encrypts each message for the one browser that subscribed (RFC 8291, aes128gcm) and signs the request with the VAPID key (RFC 8292), with nothing but `node:crypto`; no push library or paid service. Push services only see ciphertext. Sends go only to the browsers' push services (Google, Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple); any other address is refused when a device is saved. A service that answers 404 or 410 (the browser unsubscribed) makes the device be forgotten.
- **After commit.** `notify()` schedules the push with Next's `after()`, so it goes out once the response is done and any transaction has committed; a notification from a rolled-back transaction is never found, so nothing is sent for it. Outside a request (scripts, tests) it waits for the transaction's session to end.
- **Devices** (`PushDevice`, `src/lib/push/models.ts`): one row per browser (its push address and keys, a label like "Chrome on Android"), at most 8 per person. A browser belongs to whoever turned it on; when someone else signs in on it, `ServiceWorker` turns its notifications off there, so a shared phone never shows one person's updates to the next.
- **Service worker** (`public/sw.js`) shows the message with the shop's icon and a monochrome status-bar badge (`public/icons/badge-96.png`); a tap brings the shop forward on that page.
- **Checks.** `tests/web-push.test.ts` matches RFC 8291's worked example byte for byte, decrypts a message as a browser would, verifies the VAPID signature and the push-service allow-list. `tests/push.integration.test.ts`: saving and refusing devices, encrypted sends to each device, forgetting gone ones, sending only after commit, and new-order notices to order staff only. `tests/e2e/push.spec.ts`: the card at both widths. A real Chrome also received a real push through Google's service and decrypted it to the exact message.
